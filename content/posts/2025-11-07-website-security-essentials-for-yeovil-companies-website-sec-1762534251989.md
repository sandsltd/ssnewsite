### Secure Hosting and HTTPS: The First Line of Defence

- Use a reputable hosting environment with isolations, strong backups, and uptime SLAs. Reliability matters in Yeovil, especially during peak shopping seasons in Somerset when customer expectations are high.
- Enforce HTTPS with modern TLS (no deprecated ciphers, perfect forward secrecy). A secure connection builds trust with customers and is a prerequisite for PCI-DSS and GDPR readiness.
- Regularly review certificate management. Expired certificates cause outages and erode credibility faster than you’d think—particularly if your site serves local customers who expect seamless service during tasks like booking a Yeovil town centre lunch or arranging a service visit.

If you’re evaluating a partner, notice whether they treat hosting and TLS as core to the design and user experience. ### Strong Authentication and Access Control

- Implement multi-factor authentication (MFA) for all admin accounts and critical access points. MFA is one of the most effective ways to stop credential theft from turning into a breach.
- Apply principle of least privilege: staff only get access to the data and tools they need to perform their role.
- Regularly review user accounts, especially when staff leave or change roles. In a busy Yeovil business, oversight can slide—so you want a process that’s automatic and visible.

This isn’t about making things harder for your team; it’s about making it safer and more controllable. And it’s a cornerstone of GDPR compliance too, which is very real in Somerset businesses handling customer data.

### Patch Management and Vulnerability Monitoring

- Keep all software up to date. Vendors push patches after vulnerabilities are found; missing these patches leaves doors open.
- Schedule regular vulnerability scans and address high-priority findings promptly. The goal isn’t perfection; it’s continuous improvement that reduces risk over time.
- Use a managed approach to monitor third-party plugins and extensions—these are often the weakest links in a site’s defence.

If you’re wondering about DIY scanning vs professional monitoring, the difference is consistent, timely remediation and clear reporting—without pulling your IT team into every minor alert. ### Web Application Firewall and Continuous Monitoring

- Deploy a Web Application Firewall (WAF) to block common attack patterns and suspicious traffic before it reaches your site.
- Set up continuous monitoring for uptime, anomalous access, and data exfiltration indicators. Early detection helps you respond quickly and protect customer trust.
- Establish an incident response plan so your team knows what to do if something goes wrong, including notifying customers and regulators in a timely, compliant manner.

### Backups and Disaster Recovery

- Implement regular, encrypted backups stored securely off-site. Backups should cover both data and essential configuration so you can recover quickly after a breach or ransomware event.
- Test restoration regularly. A backup that can’t be restored is not really a backup. We’ve seen businesses in Somerset lose days of work because their restore process wasn’t tested in advance.
- Align your recovery RTOs and RPOs with business needs. For a Yeovil business that relies on e-commerce or appointment bookings, even tens of minutes of downtime can mean significant revenue losses.
