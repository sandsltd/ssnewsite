## Understanding What Google Actually Wants

Before diving into tactics, let's dispel a common myth: Google isn't trying to keep your website down. Its entire business model depends on showing users the most relevant, helpful results for their searches. If your website provides genuine value to searchers, Google wants to rank it highly.

Google's algorithm evaluates hundreds of ranking signals, but they broadly fall into three categories: content quality and relevance, technical performance and user experience, and site authority and trustworthiness. Master these three pillars, and you'll see your rankings improve—guaranteed.

The challenge for most business owners isn't understanding these principles in theory; it's knowing which specific actions to take and in what order. That's exactly what this guide addresses.

## 1. Conduct Thorough Keyword Research

You can't improve your Google rankings without first understanding what your customers are actually searching for. Keyword research forms the foundation of every successful SEO strategy, yet many businesses skip this crucial step.

Start by brainstorming the terms and phrases your ideal customers might use when looking for your products or services. Then validate these assumptions using tools like Google Keyword Planner, which shows you actual search volumes and competition levels for each term.

Focus on a mix of keyword types. "Head terms" like "web design" have high search volumes but fierce competition. "Long-tail keywords" like "affordable web design for small businesses in Somerset" have lower volumes but much higher conversion rates because they capture searchers with specific intent.

For local businesses, prioritising location-specific keywords often delivers the fastest results. A Yeovil-based café will find it far easier to rank for "best coffee shop in Yeovil" than competing nationally for "best coffee shop."

## 2. Create Content That Matches Search Intent

Here's a critical insight that many businesses miss: ranking well isn't about having the most keywords—it's about matching what the searcher actually wants to find.

Google categorises search intent into four types. Informational searches seek knowledge ("how to improve Google rankings"). Navigational searches look for specific websites ("Saunders Simmons Yeovil"). Commercial investigation compares options ("best web design agencies Somerset"). Transactional searches indicate readiness to purchase ("hire SEO consultant").

When creating content, ask yourself: what is someone searching this term hoping to discover? If they're searching "how much does a website cost UK," they don't want a sales pitch—they want transparent pricing information. Give them exactly what they're looking for, and Google will reward you with higher rankings.

## 3. Optimise Your Title Tags and Meta Descriptions

Your title tag is the clickable headline that appears in search results, and it's one of the most powerful on-page SEO elements you control. Every page should have a unique, descriptive title that includes your target keyword—ideally near the beginning.

Keep titles under 60 characters to prevent them being cut off in search results. Make them compelling enough to encourage clicks, but accurate enough to match the page content. "How to Improve Google Rankings: 15 Proven Strategies" is far more clickable than "Google Rankings Page."

Your meta description doesn't directly influence rankings, but it dramatically affects click-through rates—which do impact rankings. Write descriptions that sell the value of clicking through. Include your target keyword, keep it under 160 characters, and think of it as advertising copy for your page.

## 4. Structure Content With Proper Heading Hierarchy

Search engines use your heading tags (H1, H2, H3, etc.) to understand your content structure and topic hierarchy. Proper heading structure helps both Google and users quickly grasp what your page covers.

Every page should have exactly one H1 tag containing your primary keyword. Your H2 tags should outline your main sections, whilst H3 tags break these into subsections. This creates a logical, scannable structure that search engines can easily parse.

Many website platforms automatically handle H1 tags through your page title, but you need to manually structure your body content with H2 and H3 tags. Think of it like a table of contents—if someone could understand your page structure just by reading the headings, you've done it right.

## 5. Write Comprehensive, In-Depth Content

Google has consistently moved towards rewarding comprehensive content that thoroughly covers a topic. Thin, superficial content simply doesn't rank anymore—even if it's perfectly optimised.

Aim for depth over brevity. Whilst there's no magic word count, top-ranking pages for competitive terms typically contain 1,500-2,500 words. More importantly, they answer the question completely, anticipate follow-up questions, and provide actionable value.

This doesn't mean padding your content with fluff. Every paragraph should serve a purpose. Include relevant examples, case studies, statistics, and practical advice that readers can implement immediately. ## 6. Improve Your Website Loading Speed

Page speed is a confirmed ranking factor, but more importantly, it's a critical user experience element. Research shows that 53% of mobile users abandon sites that take longer than three seconds to load. If your site is slow, you're losing both rankings and customers.

Start by testing your site speed using Google PageSpeed Insights, which provides specific recommendations for improvement. Common speed issues include uncompressed images, excessive plugins, poor hosting quality, and render-blocking JavaScript.

Image optimisation often delivers the biggest speed improvements. Before uploading images, compress them using tools like TinyPNG or WebP conversion. Implement lazy loading so images only load as users scroll down the page. ## 7. Ensure Your Site Is Fully Mobile-Friendly

Google uses mobile-first indexing, meaning it predominantly evaluates the mobile version of your website when determining rankings. If your site doesn't work perfectly on smartphones and tablets, you're fighting an uphill battle.

Responsive design—where your site automatically adapts to different screen sizes—is now non-negotiable. Test your site on actual mobile devices, not just browser developer tools. Buttons should be easily tappable, text should be readable without zooming, and navigation should be intuitive on small screens.

Common mobile issues include pop-ups that can't be closed, content wider than the screen, and tiny clickable elements placed too close together. Google's Mobile-Friendly Test tool identifies these problems quickly. Understanding the [benefits of responsive web design](/blog/benefits-responsive-web-design) helps explain why mobile optimisation is so crucial for modern SEO success.

## 8. Fix Technical SEO Issues

Technical SEO problems often create invisible barriers that prevent Google from properly crawling, indexing, and ranking your content. Even brilliant content won't rank if Google can't access it properly.

Regular technical audits should check for broken links that create dead ends for users and search engines. Duplicate content across multiple URLs that confuses search engines about which version to rank. Missing or duplicate title tags and meta descriptions. Slow server response times indicating hosting problems. And crawl errors preventing Google from accessing important pages.

Google Search Console is your first line of defence here—it reports technical issues directly from Google and should be monitored monthly. Many technical problems require developer expertise to resolve properly, making this another area where [professional SEO services](https://www.saunders-simmons.co.uk/services/seo/) often prove invaluable.

## 9. Implement Strategic Internal Linking

Internal links—links between pages on your own website—help search engines understand your site structure and spread ranking authority throughout your site. Yet most businesses severely underutilise this powerful SEO technique.

When you publish new content, look for opportunities to link to it from relevant existing pages. When updating old content, add links to newer related articles. Use descriptive anchor text that tells users and search engines what they'll find when clicking through.

Strategic internal linking creates a web of related content that keeps users engaged longer and helps search engines discover and understand your most important pages. Every blog post should include 2-5 contextual internal links to related content—exactly as we've done throughout this guide.

## 10. Earn High-Quality Backlinks

Backlinks—links from other websites to yours—remain one of Google's most important ranking signals. They act as votes of confidence, with each quality link telling Google that your content is valuable and trustworthy.

However, not all links are created equal. One link from a respected industry publication carries more weight than hundreds of links from low-quality directories. Focus on earning links from relevant, authoritative sites in your industry or location.

The best link-building strategy is creating content that naturally attracts links. Original research, comprehensive guides, useful tools, and compelling infographics all tend to earn organic backlinks. Guest posting on industry blogs, building relationships with journalists, and creating local partnerships can also generate quality links.

Avoid paid link schemes, link farms, or any tactics that try to manipulate rankings artificially. Google's algorithms are sophisticated enough to detect and penalise these practices, often doing more harm than good.

## 11. Optimise for Local Search

For businesses serving specific geographic areas, local SEO can deliver faster results than competing nationally. Local optimisation helps you appear in Google's "Local Pack"—those three businesses shown with map pins above regular search results.

Start by claiming and fully optimising your Google Business Profile. Ensure your business name, address, and phone number are identical across your website and all online directories. Encourage satisfied customers to leave Google reviews. Create location-specific content that mentions the areas you serve.

For multi-location businesses, create dedicated pages for each location with unique content—not just your address swapped into a template. ## 12. Improve Your User Experience Signals

Google increasingly uses user experience signals to evaluate whether searchers find your content valuable. If users click your listing but immediately return to search results (called "pogo-sticking"), Google interprets this as your page not meeting their needs.

Reduce bounce rates by ensuring your content matches search intent, improving page load speed, and making your site easy to navigate. Increase dwell time by structuring content for readability with short paragraphs, bullet points, and relevant images. Make it easy for users to find related content through clear navigation and internal links.

These improvements simultaneously boost rankings and conversion rates—a rare win-win in digital marketing.

## 13. Update and Refresh Existing Content

Many businesses focus exclusively on creating new content whilst neglecting valuable content they've already published. Regularly updating existing pages can deliver significant ranking improvements with less effort than creating new content from scratch.

Review your existing content quarterly. Update statistics and examples to keep information current. Expand thin content with additional details and examples. Add new sections covering questions that have emerged since original publication. Improve internal linking to and from the page.

Google rewards fresh, updated content—which is exactly why we're refreshing this guide today, 168 days after original publication, with expanded strategies and current examples.

## 14. Leverage Schema Markup

Schema markup (structured data) is code that helps search engines understand your content more precisely. Whilst it doesn't directly boost rankings, it can result in rich snippets—enhanced search results with star ratings, pricing, availability, and other eye-catching details that dramatically increase click-through rates.

For local businesses, LocalBusiness schema helps you appear in map results and knowledge panels. For articles, Article schema can generate rich results showing your headline, image, and publication date directly in search results. For products, Product schema displays pricing and availability.

Implementing schema requires technical knowledge, but tools like Google's Structured Data Markup Helper simplify the process. Many modern website platforms, including WordPress with appropriate plugins, can add schema automatically.

## 15. Monitor, Measure, and Adjust

Learning how to improve Google rankings isn't a one-time project—it's an ongoing process of testing, measuring, and refining. Without tracking your progress, you're essentially optimising blind.

Set up Google Search Console and Google Analytics to monitor organic traffic, keyword rankings, click-through rates, and user behaviour. Track your positions for target keywords monthly. Measure how long users stay on your site and which pages generate the most engagement and conversions.

Review this data regularly and adjust your strategy based on what's working. If certain content types consistently perform well, create more of them. If technical issues keep appearing, address the root cause. ## Common Mistakes That Sabotage Rankings

Even with the best intentions, certain missteps can undermine your SEO efforts. Watch out for these common pitfalls.

Expecting overnight results tops the list. SEO typically requires 3-6 months to show significant impact. Businesses that abandon their strategy after a few weeks miss out on the compounding benefits that come with consistent effort.

Keyword stuffing—cramming your target keyword unnaturally throughout content—now triggers penalties rather than boosting rankings. Write naturally for humans first, ensuring keywords appear where they make sense contextually.

Neglecting mobile users remains surprisingly common despite mobile-first indexing being standard for years. Test your site on actual mobile devices regularly.

Focusing solely on rankings rather than conversions misses the entire point of SEO. A page-one ranking for an irrelevant keyword that never converts is worthless compared to a page-two ranking for a term that brings qualified leads.
