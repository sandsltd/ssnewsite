## Understanding Website Pricing Models

Before we dive into specific costs, it's important to understand that website pricing isn't one-size-fits-all. Several factors influence the final price:

- **Complexity**: A five-page brochure site costs less than a 50-page ecommerce platform
- **Design requirements**: Template-based designs are cheaper than fully custom designs
- **Functionality**: Custom features like booking systems or member portals increase costs
- **Content creation**: Professional copywriting and photography add to the budget
- **Ongoing maintenance**: Some agencies include hosting and updates; others charge separately

Most UK web design agencies offer three main pricing approaches: template-based websites, semi-custom designs, or fully bespoke development. Each has its place depending on your business needs and budget.

## Brochure Website Costs (£800–£5,000)

A brochure website is typically a straightforward informational site with 5-10 pages covering your services, about page, and contact details. These are ideal for small businesses, tradespeople, or local services that need an online presence without complex functionality.

### Budget Brochure Sites (£800–£1,500)

At the lower end, you're looking at template-based WordPress designs with minimal customisation. These typically include:

- 5-7 standard pages
- Template-based design with your branding
- Basic SEO setup
- Mobile-responsive layout
- Contact form

This option works well if you're just starting out or need something simple quickly. However, you'll have limited flexibility and your site may look similar to others using the same template.

### Professional Brochure Sites (£2,000–£5,000)

Mid-range brochure sites offer more customisation and professional polish. Expect:

- 8-12 custom pages
- Bespoke design elements tailored to your brand
- Professional copywriting assistance
- Enhanced SEO optimisation
- Integration with business tools (email marketing, CRM)
- Training on how to update content yourself

This is the sweet spot for established small businesses that want a professional online presence that properly represents their brand. ## Ecommerce Website Costs (£3,000–£25,000+)

If you're selling products online, you'll need an ecommerce platform. When considering how much does a website cost in the UK in 2026 for ecommerce, expect to pay more than a brochure site due to additional functionality requirements.

### Entry-Level Ecommerce (£3,000–£7,000)

Suitable for businesses with a smaller product range (under 100 products), these sites typically use platforms like WooCommerce or Shopify and include:

- Product catalogue setup
- Shopping cart and checkout functionality
- Payment gateway integration (Stripe, PayPal)
- Basic inventory management
- Mobile-responsive design
- SSL certificate for secure transactions

This option works well for craftspeople, small retailers, or businesses testing online sales for the first time.

### Mid-Range Ecommerce (£8,000–£15,000)

For growing online businesses with larger inventories and more complex needs:

- Custom ecommerce design
- Advanced product filtering and search
- Multiple payment options
- Integration with accounting software
- Email marketing automation
- Customer account areas
- Professional product photography assistance

Most established retailers fit into this category, needing robust functionality that can handle hundreds of products and significant traffic.

### Enterprise Ecommerce (£15,000–£25,000+)

Large-scale online retailers require sophisticated solutions:

- Fully bespoke design and development
- Custom functionality and integrations
- Multi-currency and multi-language support
- Advanced inventory management
- B2B wholesale capabilities
- Complex shipping calculations
- Comprehensive analytics and reporting

These projects typically take 3-6 months to complete and require ongoing maintenance and optimisation.

## Custom Web Application Costs (£10,000–£50,000+)

If your business needs go beyond a standard website—perhaps you need a booking system, member portal, or proprietary software—you're looking at [custom software development](https://www.saunders-simmons.co.uk/custom-software-development/).

Custom applications are priced based on development time. UK web developers typically charge £50-£150 per hour, depending on expertise. A moderately complex web application requiring 200 hours of development would cost £10,000-£30,000.

Common custom web applications include:

- **Booking and scheduling systems** (£8,000-£20,000): For healthcare providers, beauty salons, or professional services
- **Member portals** (£10,000-£25,000): For gyms, associations, or subscription businesses
- **Property portals** (£15,000-£40,000): For estate agents or holiday letting agencies
- **Learning management systems** (£20,000-£50,000+): For educational institutions or training providers

These projects require detailed planning, and the investment reflects the bespoke nature of the solution perfectly tailored to your business processes.

## Ongoing Website Costs

When budgeting for a website, don't forget ongoing expenses:

- **Hosting**: £10-£100+ per month depending on traffic and requirements
- **Domain name**: £10-£20 per year
- **SSL certificate**: Often included with hosting
- **Maintenance and updates**: £50-£300+ per month
- **Content updates**: £40-£80 per hour for professional updates
- **SEO and digital marketing**: £500-£2,000+ per month

Many businesses overlook these costs, but they're essential for keeping your site secure, fast, and effective. We recommend budgeting at least £100-£200 monthly for basic maintenance and hosting.

## What Affects Website Costs in 2026?

Several trends are influencing website pricing this year:

**Increased focus on accessibility**: With the Equality Act requiring accessible websites, proper WCAG compliance adds development time but is essential for avoiding legal issues and reaching all customers.

**AI integration**: More businesses want AI chatbots and personalisation features, which increase development costs but improve user experience.

**Advanced security requirements**: With cyber threats growing, robust security measures are non-negotiable, adding to both initial development and ongoing costs.

**Performance optimisation**: Google's Core Web Vitals mean faster-loading sites rank better, requiring more technical optimisation work.

## Making Your Decision

Website costs vary enormously because every business has different needs. A £1,500 template site might be perfect for a local tradesperson, whilst a £20,000 custom ecommerce platform could be essential for a growing retailer.

The key is matching your investment to your business goals. Ask yourself:

- How important is your website to your business model?
- What return on investment do you expect?
- Will you need regular updates and new features?
- Does your industry demand a premium online presence?

Your website is often the first impression potential customers have of your business. Investing appropriately in professional design and development pays dividends through increased credibility, better user experience, and ultimately more conversions.
