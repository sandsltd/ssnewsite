If you run a local business in Yeovil, Somerset, or anywhere in the UK, appearing in Google Maps results can be a game-changer. When potential customers search for services "near me" or in a specific location, the map pack—those three businesses displayed above organic search results—captures the majority of clicks.

Understanding how to rank higher on Google Maps isn't just about technical SEO; it's about demonstrating to Google that your business is relevant, trustworthy, and deserves to be shown to local searchers. In this guide, we'll walk you through the essential steps to improve your Google Maps ranking and attract more local customers.

## Why Google Maps Rankings Matter for Local Businesses

Before diving into the tactics, it's worth understanding why Google Maps visibility is so crucial. The map pack appears for most local searches—whether someone's looking for "web design agency near me" or "plumber in Yeovil"—and these results receive significantly more clicks than traditional organic listings below them.

For brick-and-mortar businesses, service providers, and local agencies like ours, ranking in the top three map results can drive substantial foot traffic, phone calls, and enquiries. Unlike traditional [SEO services](/services/seo/), which can take months to show results, improvements to your Google Maps presence can sometimes yield results in weeks.

## Understanding Google's Local Ranking Factors

Google uses three primary factors to determine which businesses appear in map results: relevance, distance, and prominence. Let's break down each one:

**Relevance** refers to how well your business matches what someone is searching for. If you run a web design agency, you want to appear when people search for web design services, not unrelated queries.

**Distance** measures how far your business is from the searcher's location. This is largely outside your control, but optimising your service areas can help you appear for searches in nearby towns.

**Prominence** reflects how well-known your business is. Google considers factors like reviews, citations, links, and overall online presence to assess prominence.

## Claim and Verify Your Google Business Profile

The foundation of how to rank higher on Google Maps starts with claiming your Google Business Profile (formerly Google My Business). If you haven't done this yet, head to the Google Business Profile website and follow the verification process.

Once claimed, complete every section of your profile thoroughly. Google favours complete profiles, and customers appreciate having all the information they need at their fingertips. Include your business name, address, phone number, website URL, business hours, and business category.

Choosing the right primary category is particularly important. Select the most specific category that describes your core service. You can add secondary categories, but your primary category should be laser-focused on what you do best.

## Optimise Your Business Information

Consistency is crucial when it comes to your business information. Your Name, Address, and Phone number (NAP) should be identical across your Google Business Profile, website, and all online directories.

Write a compelling business description that naturally incorporates relevant keywords without stuffing. Describe what makes your business unique, the areas you serve, and the problems you solve for customers. For example, we might mention that we're a "full-service digital agency in Yeovil, Somerset, specialising in web design, SEO, and software development for UK businesses."

Add high-quality photos regularly. Businesses with photos receive 42% more requests for driving directions and 35% more click-throughs to their websites. Include exterior and interior shots, team photos, and images of your work or products.

## Gather and Manage Customer Reviews

Reviews are one of the most influential factors in how to rank higher on Google Maps. They signal to Google that your business is active, trusted, and provides good service. More importantly, they influence potential customers' decisions to choose your business over competitors.

Develop a systematic approach to requesting reviews from satisfied customers. Send follow-up emails after completing a project, include review requests in your invoicing process, or simply ask in person. Make it easy by providing a direct link to your Google review page.

Respond to every review—both positive and negative. Thank customers for positive feedback and address concerns raised in negative reviews professionally. This demonstrates that you value customer feedback and actively manage your online reputation.

Never buy fake reviews or incentivise customers to leave positive reviews in exchange for discounts. Google can detect these practices and may penalise your business by lowering your rankings or removing your profile entirely.

## Build Local Citations and Ensure NAP Consistency

Citations are online mentions of your business name, address, and phone number on other websites. These might be business directories, review sites, or local websites. Citations help validate your business's existence and location to Google.

Focus on quality over quantity. Start with major UK business directories like Yell, Thomson Local, and Scoot, then expand to industry-specific directories relevant to your sector. Ensure your NAP information is consistent across all citations.

## Create Location-Specific Content

Your website plays a supporting role in your Google Maps rankings. Creating location-specific content helps Google understand where you operate and what you offer. If you serve multiple areas, consider creating dedicated pages for each location.

For instance, we've created pages specifically about [web design in Yeovil](/web-design-yeovil/) and services across Somerset. These pages signal to Google that we're relevant for local searches in these areas.

Include location keywords naturally in your content, meta titles, and descriptions. Write blog posts about local events, news, or issues that affect your target audience. This demonstrates your connection to the local community.

## Build Quality Local Backlinks

Links from other local websites tell Google that your business is an established part of the local community. Seek opportunities to earn links from local news sites, chambers of commerce, local blogs, and complementary businesses.

Sponsor local events, participate in community initiatives, or offer your expertise to local organisations. These activities not only build your reputation but often result in valuable local backlinks that boost your prominence in Google's eyes.

## Optimise for Mobile Users

The majority of local searches happen on mobile devices, often from people actively looking for a business to visit or call. Ensure your website is fully responsive and loads quickly on mobile devices.

Your [web design](/services/web-design/) should prioritise mobile users with clear calls-to-action, click-to-call buttons, and easy navigation. A poor mobile experience can undermine your Google Maps visibility, as Google considers user experience signals in its ranking algorithm.

## Use Google Posts Regularly

Google Posts allow you to share updates, offers, events, and news directly on your Google Business Profile. These posts appear in your profile and can increase engagement, which signals to Google that your business is active and relevant.

Post regularly—at least once per week—with a mix of content types. Share company news, special offers, blog posts, or upcoming events. Include clear calls-to-action and relevant keywords naturally.

## Monitor Your Insights and Adjust Your Strategy

Google Business Profile provides valuable insights into how customers find and interact with your listing. Review these metrics regularly to understand which searches trigger your profile, how customers contact you, and where customers are located.

Use this data to refine your strategy. If you're appearing for irrelevant searches, adjust your business description or categories. If customers from a particular area frequently engage with your profile, consider creating more location-specific content for that area.

## Track Your Local Rankings

Monitor your Google Maps rankings for your target keywords and locations. This helps you understand whether your optimisation efforts are working and identify opportunities for improvement.

Several tools can help you track local rankings, including BrightLocal, Whitespark, and Local Falcon. These tools show your visibility in different locations, helping you understand your true local reach.

## Taking Action to Improve Your Google Maps Visibility

Learning how to rank higher on Google Maps requires consistent effort across multiple factors—from optimising your Google Business Profile to building citations, earning reviews, and creating location-specific content. Unlike traditional SEO, local search optimisation often delivers faster results, making it an excellent investment for businesses serving local markets.

Start with the fundamentals: claim and optimise your profile, gather reviews, and ensure NAP consistency. Then expand your efforts to include regular posts, [local content creation](/blog/local-seo-guide-small-businesses-uk), and link building. Monitor your progress and adjust your strategy based on what works for your specific business and location.
